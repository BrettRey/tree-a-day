## Shortlist

1. **"to give A.I. access to all of its 51 million citizens"** (Beth Simone Noveck, NYT opinion, 6 Oct 2026, quoted by Mark Liberman, "An ominous syntactic ambiguity", Language Log, 7 Oct 2026, https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=74365). Secondary: the NYT page was walled. Full sentence: "In return for providing government funding, South Korea is securing contracts with multiple tech and telecom companies to give A.I. access to all of its 51 million citizens." Ditransitive (the AI gets access to the citizens) against monotransitive (the citizens get AI access): a pair image. Suggested for Monday 2 November.
2. **"... is too early to call"** headlines on election night. Example: AP, 4 Mar 2026, "GOP headed to runoff while Democrats are too early to call in pivotal Texas US Senate primaries" (verified on a syndicated copy). A hollow infinitival in which *early* isn't predicated of the subject. Best drafted on Wednesday 4 November from a live headline.
3. **"FIND YOUR FILTHY COMFORTABLE"** (Charles Schwab display ad, captured in Language Log, 9 Oct 2026, https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=74386; verified from the ad image). *your filthy comfortable* is an AdjP after a determiner, so CGEL's fused Mod-Head against the ordinary Head of *Fusion of functions revisited*, a follow-up to the Halloween poll. Suggested for Friday 6 November.
4. **"AI is so AI"** (Guardian, Pass notes, 24 Sept 2026, verified). The same form as an N in the subject and after degree *so*. Category against function. Avoid pairing it with no. 1 in the same week.
5. **"Everyone is maxxing something…what's yours?"** and **"there are now a lot of maxxings"** (Dictionary.com, 2026 Word of the Year, 25 Oct 2026, verified). One spelling, V and N.
6. **"Is it a book that you would even wish your wife or your servants to read?"** (*Lady Chatterley* prosecutor; verdict 2 Nov 1960). The wording conflicts between sources (*that* or not); needs Rolph, *The Trial of Lady Chatterley* (1961), p. 17, which is borrow-only on archive.org.
7. **"Whoever dies with honor dies well."** Brett's find; drafted in `2026-10-31-whoever-dies/`.
